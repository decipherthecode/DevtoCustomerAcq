## The 7 simple rules

1. **Go in order.** Start at Section 1 and finish at Section 21. Sections 22 and 23 are a sample and a change log. Later sections build on earlier ones.
2. **Answer in plain words.** Short sentences are best. Nobody is grading your writing.
3. **Look at the example first.** Every section has a 💡 **Example**. Copy its *style*, never its words.
4. **Replace every `[FILL IN]`.** Write your answer in its place. Never leave one behind.
5. **If you don't know, don't guess.** Write `UNKNOWN - will find out by [date] - owner [name]`. A clear "unknown" is better than a made-up answer.
6. **Use numbers where asked.** "Many customers" is not a number. "40 customers" is.
7. **Update the status tag.** Every section starts with a status line. Change it as you go:
   - `⬜ NOT STARTED` → `🟨 IN PROGRESS` → `🟩 DONE` → `✅ APPROVED`

# APPENDIX A: COMMON MISTAKES (DO NOT DO THESE)

1. Writing goals with no numbers ("improve retention a lot").
2. Counting sign-ups as success instead of customers who stay.
3. Adding tracking after launch, so early data is lost.
4. Using one overall churn number and ignoring cohorts and segments.
5. Fixing symptoms (discounts) instead of causes (customers never reach the aha moment).
6. Making cancellation hard to find or confusing.
7. Letting AI send messages about money or complaints without human approval.
8. Never talking to customers who left.
9. Changing five things at once, so you can't tell what worked.
10. Leaving "owner" blank, which means no one does the task.
11. Setting up automation before you understand your first 100 customers.
12. Treating this document as finished. Review it regularly.

# APPENDIX B: GLOSSARY (PLAIN-ENGLISH WORDS)

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **Retention** | Customers who keep using or paying |
| **Churn** | Customers who stop. The opposite of retention |
| **Activation** | A new customer doing the key thing that shows value |
| **Aha moment** | The moment a customer first sees it is useful |
| **Cohort** | A group of customers who joined in the same period (e.g., "March sign-ups") |
| **Segment** | A type of customer (e.g., students vs. teachers) |
| **NPS** | Score from 0-10: "How likely are you to recommend us?" |
| **LTV (lifetime value)** | Total money an average customer brings before leaving |
| **Onboarding** | Helping new customers get started |
| **Win-back** | Bringing back customers who left |
| **Champion** | A very happy customer who helps spread the word |
| **Funnel** | The steps from first visit to becoming a happy, active customer |
| **Beta** | An early test version used by a few people |
| **Dashboard** | A page showing your key numbers in one place |
| **Go / No-go** | The final yes or no decision |
| **AI agent** | A software helper that carries out tasks, with human checking where required |
