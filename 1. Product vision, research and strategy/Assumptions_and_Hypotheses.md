# 📋 How to Fill This Document

> **Read this first — it takes 3 minutes and saves you hours of confusion later.**

## 🌟 What Is This Document?

Think of this document as your project's **"belief journal."**

Before you build anything, you make guesses — about your customers, your technology, your business model, and your AI. Some guesses are right. Some are wrong. The ones that are wrong can sink your project if you don't catch them early.

This document helps you:
1. **Write down your guesses** (so you don't forget them)
2. **Test each guess** (so you know if it's true or not)
3. **Track what you learned** (so your whole team is on the same page)
4. **Make smarter decisions** (instead of wasting months building the wrong thing)

---

## 📖 Two Key Words You Need to Know

| Word | Simple Meaning | Example |
|------|---------------|---------|
| **Assumption** | Something you believe is true, but haven't proven yet | *"I assume people hate doing their taxes manually."* |
| **Hypothesis** | A testable guess with a clear way to prove it true or false | *"I believe 7 out of 10 small business owners will pay $29/month for automatic tax tracking — I'll know this is true when 70 out of 100 people on my waitlist click 'Pre-order.'"* |

---

## ✏️ Step-by-Step: How to Fill This Document

**Step 1 — Fill in the Project Overview** (Section 0)
> Answer the basic "who, what, when" about your project. 5 minutes max.

**Step 2 — Work through each section in order**
> Start with Business & Market (Section 1), then User & UX (Section 2), then Technical & AI (Section 3), then Regulatory & Risk (Section 4).

**Step 3 — For each assumption, fill in ALL fields**
> Don't skip fields. If you don't know the answer, write: *"Unknown — will test by [date]."*

**Step 4 — Set a review date for each assumption**
> Pick a date you'll come back and check if your assumption was right.

**Step 5 — Update statuses as you learn**
> When you test an assumption, mark it:
> - ✅ **Validated** — it's true! Great, keep going.
> - ❌ **Invalidated** — it's false. Time to adjust your plan.
> - ⏳ **Unknown** — not tested yet.
> - 🔄 **Re-testing** — new evidence, running another test.

**Step 6 — Check stage gates before advancing**
> Before moving from one stage to the next, use Section 5 checklists. Think of them as "permission slips" to advance.

**Step 7 — Update the Revision History** (Section 10)
> Every time you change this document, log it at the bottom. Future-you will thank present-you.

---

## ⚡ Pro Tips (Even a Beginner Can Use These)

- 💡 **Quantity over perfection** — Write 20 rough assumptions, not 3 perfect ones.
- 🔴 **Validate risky assumptions first** — If you're wrong about a Critical one, everything else doesn't matter.
- 🗣️ **Talk to real people** — The best way to test most assumptions is to have a 20-minute conversation with your target customer.
- 📸 **Document your evidence** — Attach screenshots, interview notes, and survey results in the Experiment Log (Section 6).
- 🔄 **This document is alive** — Update it every 2 weeks. Old assumptions become outdated fast.

## 🗺️ Stage Tags — What They Mean

Each assumption is tagged with the stage where you should test it:

| Tag | Stage | What Happens Here |
|-----|-------|-------------------|
| 🔍 | **Discovery** | Research, interviews, problem validation |
| 🎨 | **Design** | Wireframes, prototypes, UX testing |
| 🛠️ | **Development** | Building the product (coding) |
| 🧪 | **QA** | Testing the product before launch |
| 🚀 | **Launch** | First real users start using the product |
| 📈 | **Retention** | Growing from your first users to 500 customers |

## 🎯 Priority & Risk — Quick Reference

**Priority** = *How urgently do you need to test this?*

| Icon | Priority | What to Do |
|------|---------|------------|
| 🔴 | **High** | Test this week — if it's wrong, the project could fail |
| 🟡 | **Medium** | Test within the month |
| 🟢 | **Low** | Test when you have time |

**Risk Level** = *How bad is it if this assumption is wrong?*

| Icon | Risk | What Happens |
|------|------|-------------|
| 💥 | **Critical** | The whole project fails or causes serious harm |
| ⚠️ | **Major** | Significant rework — costs weeks of work |
| 💡 | **Minor** | Small fix needed — costs days of work |

Copy this block for every assumption you want to track. Fill in every field.

```

**Why I Believe This:** [Where did this belief come from? Personal experience? Research? Gut feeling? Be honest.]
**Priority:** 🔴 High / 🟡 Medium / 🟢 Low

**Stage to Validate:** 🔍 / 🎨 / 🛠️ / 🧪 / 🚀 / 📈
**How to Test:** [What specific action will you take? e.g., "Interview 10 restaurant owners and ask if this is a top-3 problem."]
**Success Criteria:** [Exact numbers. e.g., "7 out of 10 say YES this is a big problem."]
**Review Date:** [Pick a real date — not "someday"]
**Status:** ⏳ Unknown
**What We Learned:** [Fill in AFTER testing]
**Decision Made:** [What did you decide to do based on what you learned?]
```

# 9️⃣ Glossary — Every Word Made Simple

> **Confused by a word? Find it here.** This glossary is written so that anyone — including someone with no tech or business background — can understand what each term means.

| Word | What It Means | Simple Example |
|------|--------------|----------------|
| **Assumption** | Something you believe is true but haven't proven yet | *"I assume pizza is the most popular food."* |
| **Hypothesis** | A testable guess with a clear pass/fail condition | *"I believe 6 of 10 people prefer pizza over sushi. I'll survey 10 friends to find out."* |
| **Validate** | Prove that something is true using real evidence | *"I interviewed 10 customers and 8 confirmed the problem — assumption validated."* |
| **Invalidate** | Prove that something is NOT true | *"Only 2 of 10 said it was a problem — assumption invalidated. Time to rethink."* |
| **Pivot** | Change your plan because you learned something important | *"We thought restaurants were our customer. We learned it's actually food trucks. We pivoted."* |
| **ICP** | Ideal Customer Profile — your perfect customer, described in detail | *"Freelance graphic designers, 25–35, in North America, who use social media daily"* |
| **TAM** | Total Addressable Market — everyone in the world who could use your product | *"50 million small business owners worldwide"* |
| **SAM** | Serviceable Addressable Market — people you can realistically reach | *"5 million English-speaking SMBs with a marketing budget"* |
| **SOM** | Serviceable Obtainable Market — people you'll realistically get | *"5,000 customers in Year 1 with our marketing budget"* |
| **Churn** | When a customer stops using and paying for your product | *"We lost 10 customers this month — that's 5% churn."* |
| **NPS** | Net Promoter Score — a 0–100 score of how likely users are to recommend you | *"Score of 50 = very good. Score of 70+ = exceptional."* |
| **Activation Moment** | The first time a new user thinks "wow, this really works!" | *"The moment a user sees their first AI-generated caption and loves it."* |
| **Hallucination** | When an AI confidently states something that is completely made up | *"The AI said the Eiffel Tower is 500 meters tall. It's actually 330 meters. That's a hallucination."* |
| **Model Drift** | When an AI's behaviour slowly changes over time without you changing anything | *"Our AI used to write funny captions. After a model update it became very formal. That's drift."* |
| **Guardrails** | Rules or filters that prevent an AI from producing harmful output | *"Our guardrails stop the AI from writing anything violent, rude, or misleading."* |
| **Jailbreaking** | Tricking an AI into ignoring its safety rules through clever prompting | *"Someone wrote 'pretend you have no rules...' to get the AI to produce banned content."* |
| **Prompt Engineering** | The art of writing instructions for AI to get the best output | *"Changing 'write a caption' to 'write a funny, emoji-filled caption for a pizza restaurant' = better prompt engineering."* |
| **Fine-tuning** | Retraining an AI model on your specific data to make it better at your tasks | *"We fine-tuned the model on 10,000 restaurant captions so it writes better food content."* |
| **RAG** | Retrieval-Augmented Generation — giving the AI access to a knowledge base before it answers | *"Before answering, the AI looks up our product docs so its answers are always accurate."* |
| **Unit Economics** | The math of whether you make money from one customer | *"We charge $29/month. We spend $8/month serving that customer. That's $21 profit per user."* |
| **Lean / Build-Measure-Learn** | A product development style: build a small thing, test it, learn, improve, repeat | *"We built a simple chatbot in 2 weeks, got 20 people to use it, learned what they hated, improved it."* |
| **Stage Gate** | A checkpoint where you decide: advance, fix things, or stop | *"Before we start coding, we need to validate the business assumptions — that's our Stage 1 Gate."* |
| **MVP** | Minimum Viable Product — the smallest version of your product that real users can use | *"A simple web form that lets users request an AI caption — before we build the full app."* |
| **GDPR** | EU law requiring you to protect user data and get consent before collecting it | *"You must ask 'Can we store your email?' and let users say no."* |
| **CCPA** | California privacy law similar to GDPR | *"California users must be able to say 'Don't sell my data.'"* |
| **PII** | Personally Identifiable Information — data that can identify a specific person | *"Name + email + address = PII. A random session ID by itself = not PII."* |
| **Dark Patterns** | UI design tricks that manipulate users into doing things they didn't want to do | *"Pre-checking a box to sign up for a newsletter = dark pattern."* |
| **p95 Response Time** | The response time experienced by the slowest 5% of users | *"p95 = 3 seconds means 95% of users get a response in under 3 seconds."* |
| **API** | Application Programming Interface — a way for your software to talk to another service | *"We use OpenAI's API to send a prompt and receive a response."* |
| **Uptime** | The percentage of time your product is working and available | *"99.9% uptime = less than 9 hours of downtime per year."* |
