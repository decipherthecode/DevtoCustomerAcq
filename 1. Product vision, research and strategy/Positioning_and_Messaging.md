# 📖 How to Fill This Document

**Who this is for:** Founders, solo builders, and small teams building an AI-powered SaaS or mobile app.

**What this document does:** It tells the world (and your own team) *who* the product is for, *what problem* it solves, *why* yours is the right solution, and *how* you will say that in every headline, email, and pitch deck.

**How long it takes:** About 2–4 hours for a first draft. You will revisit it before every major launch milestone.

**Step-by-step instructions:**

1. **Read the whole document once** before writing anything. Get the big picture first.
2. **Fill every field that has a `▶`** marker. Fields marked `[REQUIRED]` must be filled before sharing the document. Fields marked `[OPTIONAL]` add depth but can be done later.
3. **Use the hints** (lines that start with 💡) to understand what a field is asking.
4. **Use the examples** (lines that start with ✏️) as inspiration — replace the example text with your own words.
5. **Delete placeholder text** (anything inside `[ ]` brackets) when you are done filling that field.
6. **Do not skip the "Competitive Landscape" section** — even if you think you have no competitors, you do. Every customer is currently solving their problem *somehow*.
7. **Revisit and update** this document at each milestone: Pre-Launch → Beta → Public Launch → 100 Customers → 500 Customers.
8. **Share it** with everyone who writes copy — developers naming features, marketers writing ads, support agents answering tickets. One source of truth.

> **🧒 Kid-friendly tip:** Think of this document like the label on a cereal box. It tells people what's inside, why it's good for them, and why they should pick it over every other box on the shelf. Your job is to write the best label possible!

## Appendix A — Glossary (For First-Time Founders)

> Use this to understand terms in the document.

| Term | Plain-English Definition |
|---|---|
| **ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)** | A detailed description of the one type of customer who gets the most value from your product and is easiest to sell to |
| **Value Proposition** | The single most compelling reason someone should buy your product instead of everything else |
| **Positioning** | How your product sits in the customer's mind relative to alternatives — the "slot" you own in their brain |
| **Messaging** | The actual words you use to communicate your positioning — headlines, taglines, elevator pitches |
| **GTM (Go-to-Market)** | The plan for how you will reach, attract, and convert customers |
| **Conversion Rate** | The % of people who take the action you want (e.g., sign up) out of all people who see the opportunity |
| **Elevator Pitch** | A 30-second verbal description of your product — short enough to say in an elevator ride |
| **NPS (Net Promoter Score)** | A 0–100 score measuring customer loyalty; calculated by asking "How likely are you to recommend us?" |
| **Objection** | A reason a potential customer gives for NOT buying — usually a fear, doubt, or misunderstanding |
| **Social Proof** | Evidence that other people trust and use your product (quotes, user counts, press mentions) |
| **Aha Moment** | The specific action in your product that makes a user think "I get it — this is worth it" |
| **Retention** | The % of users who keep using the product over time (opposite of "churn") |
| **Build in Public** | The practice of sharing your startup journey openly on social media to attract an audience before launch |
| **Jobs to Be Done (JTBD)** | A framework: people don't buy products — they "hire" them to accomplish a specific job in their life |
