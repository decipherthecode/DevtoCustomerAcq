# 📋 How to Fill This Document — START HERE

> 👋 **Hi there!** Before you fill anything in, read this section first. It will take you 5 minutes and save you hours of confusion later. Think of this document as your product's **scoreboard** — just like in a soccer game, everyone on the team needs to know the score, what counts as winning, and how much time is left. That's exactly what this document does for your AI product.

---

## # Step 1 — Fill in the Project Info Block (⏱ 5 minutes)
At the very top of the document (Section 1 below), fill in:
- **Project Name** → What is your product called? (e.g., "SmartReply AI")
- **Version** → Start with `v1.0`. Change to `v1.1` every time you do a major update.
- **Document Owner** → Who is responsible for keeping this doc updated? Put your name.
- **Date Created** → Today's date.
- **Last Updated** → Change this every time you edit anything.

## # Step 2 — Set Your North Star Metric (⏱ 10 minutes)
Your **North Star Metric** is the ONE number that, if it goes up, means your whole product is getting better. You can only pick ONE.

**How to choose it:** Ask yourself: *"What single number proves that real users are getting real value from our AI product?"*

| If your product does... | Your North Star might be... |
|---|---|
| Saves people time | Minutes saved per user per week |
| Helps people write better | AI-generated content accepted per user |
| Automates tasks | Tasks completed per user per week |
| Connects buyers and sellers | Successful matches per week |

## # Step 3 — Write Your OKRs (⏱ 30 minutes)
OKR stands for **Objective + Key Results**. Think of it like this:
- **Objective** = Where do you want to GO? (written like a dream, inspiring, not a number)
- **Key Results** = How will you KNOW you got there? (always a number with a target)

**Example:**
> **Objective:** "Make our AI assistant something people can't imagine working without."
> **Key Result 1:** Achieve 40% Weekly Active User rate by end of Q2
> **Key Result 2:** Reach NPS score of +45 from beta users

## # Step 4 — Fill Metrics Phase by Phase (⏱ Ongoing)
Don't fill everything at once! Fill only the phase you're currently in.
- **Phase 1** → Fill when you start building
- **Phase 2** → Fill when you start testing
- **Phase 3** → Fill when you launch beta
- **Phase 4** → Fill when you go public

## # Step 5 — Update the Traffic Light Status (⏱ 5 min / week)
Every metric has a **Status** column. Use these every Monday:

| Emoji | Meaning | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 | **On Track** | You're meeting or beating your target |
| 🟡 | **At Risk** | You're close but slipping — act now! |
| 🔴 | **Off Track** | You're missing the target — this needs a fix this week |
| ⚪ | **Not Started** | This phase hasn't begun yet |

## # Step 6 — Update the Weekly Dashboard Every Monday (⏱ 10 min)
Every Monday morning, fill in the "Weekly Review Dashboard" section. This takes 10 minutes and keeps your whole team aligned. If you're a solo founder, it keeps future-you aligned with past-you.

## # Step 7 — Complete the 500-Customer Milestone Card
This is your **"finish line"** for early traction. When customer #500 signs up, work through this card together as a team. It's a moment worth celebrating! 🎉

## # Step 8 — Fill the Post-Mortem After Each Phase
After every phase ends, spend 60 minutes as a team filling the Retrospective section. This is where institutional knowledge lives. Across 100s of projects, this section alone will save you thousands of hours.

## 📖 APPENDIX — Metric Glossary

> 💡 This glossary explains every important term in this document in plain language. Share this with anyone who's new to the team.

| Term | Plain-English Definition |
|---|---|
| **AARRR / Pirate Metrics** | A framework for startup growth: Acquisition → Activation → Retention → Referral → Revenue |
| **Activation** | The moment a new user experiences the core value of your product for the first time |
| **ARPU** | Average Revenue Per User — total revenue divided by number of users |
| **Bias (AI)** | When an AI treats different groups of people unfairly due to patterns in its training data |
| **CAC** | Customer Acquisition Cost — how much you spend to get one new paying customer |
| **Churn** | When a customer cancels their subscription or stops using your product |
| **CSAT** | Customer Satisfaction Score — users rate their experience on a 1–5 scale |
| **DAU / MAU** | Daily Active Users / Monthly Active Users — a ratio showing how "sticky" your product is |
| **E2E Test** | End-to-End Test — automated test that simulates a full user journey |
| **Eval Dataset** | A fixed set of test inputs used to measure AI quality — never used in training |
| **F1 Score** | Combined measure of an AI classifier's precision and recall (0–1, higher is better) |
| **Guardrail** | A safety filter that prevents an AI from producing harmful or inappropriate output |
| **Hallucination** | When an AI confidently states something that is factually incorrect |
| **HEART** | Google UX framework: Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, Task Success |
| **KPI** | Key Performance Indicator — a measurable value showing how well you're achieving an objective |
| **LTV** | Lifetime Value — total expected revenue from one customer over their entire relationship with you |
| **Model Drift** | When AI quality degrades over time because real-world data has shifted from training data |
| **MRR** | Monthly Recurring Revenue — predictable monthly income from subscriptions |
| **NPS** | Net Promoter Score — measures how likely customers are to recommend you (−100 to +100) |
| **North Star Metric** | The single most important number for your product's success |
| **OKR** | Objective + Key Results — goal-setting framework used by Google, Spotify, etc. |
| **P50 / P95 / P99 Latency** | Response time at the 50th, 95th, and 99th percentile — shows typical, slow, and worst-case speed |
| **Prompt Injection** | A type of attack where users try to manipulate an AI by hiding instructions in their input |
| **RAG Status** | Red / Amber / Green — traffic light system for project health |
| **Retention** | The % of users who come back and keep using your product after their first session |
| **SLA** | Service Level Agreement — your commitment to users about uptime, response time, etc. |
| **Sprint** | A 2-week development cycle where the team commits to specific deliverables |
| **TTFV** | Time to First Value — how long until a new user experiences their first success |
| **Unit Economics** | The revenue and costs directly tied to one unit (usually one customer) |
| **Viral Coefficient (K)** | Average number of new users each existing user brings in |
