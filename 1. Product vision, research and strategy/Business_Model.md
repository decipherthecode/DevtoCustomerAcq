### 5.2 AI Agent Workflow Map

> **# How to fill this:**
> For your most important AI-powered feature, describe the step-by-step process the AI follows. Think of it like a recipe — what does the AI do first, second, third?

**Feature: [Name of your most important AI workflow]**

```
Step 1: TRIGGER
   → [What starts the AI agent? e.g., "User connects GitHub account"]

Step 2: DATA COLLECTION
   → [What data does the AI gather? e.g., "Reads open issues, PRs, commits from the last 7 days"]

Step 3: AI PROCESSING
   → [What does the AI analyse or decide? e.g., "Scores each item by urgency × importance using an LLM"]

Step 4: ACTION / OUTPUT
   → [What does the AI produce or do? e.g., "Creates a prioritised task list in Notion and sends a Slack message"]

Step 5: HUMAN REVIEW (Optional)
   → [Does the user review/approve anything? e.g., "User can accept, edit, or reject each suggested task"]

Step 6: LEARNING / FEEDBACK LOOP
   → [Does the AI improve over time? e.g., "AI learns which tasks the user accepts vs. rejects and adjusts future scoring"]
```

> 💡 Example:
>
> **Feature: Morning Briefing Agent**
> ```
> Step 1: TRIGGER → Runs every day at 7:00 AM (user's local time)
> Step 2: DATA   → Pulls unread Slack messages, open GitHub issues, calendar events
> Step 3: AI     → Claude API ranks items by urgency; groups related tasks; drops items older than 14 days
> Step 4: OUTPUT → Sends a 5-bullet Slack message: "Here are your top 3 tasks for today + 2 things to ignore"
> Step 5: REVIEW → User taps ✅ Done or 🔄 Reschedule on each item
> Step 6: LEARN  → Feedback stored; agent deprioritises recurring "ignored" items automatically
> ```

## 17. Glossary

> **# Note:** This section explains the business/tech terms used in this document. If you already know all these terms, skip it. If you're unsure about any term, look it up here first.

| Term | Plain English Definition |
|---|---|
| **AI Agent** | A software program that can take actions on its own — like browsing the web, sending messages, or updating files — based on a goal you give it |
| **ARPU** | Average Revenue Per User — how much money each customer pays you on average per month |
| **CAC** | Customer Acquisition Cost — how much money you spend on average to win one new customer |
| **Churn Rate** | The percentage of customers who cancel their subscription in a given month |
| **GTM** | Go-To-Market — the plan for how you'll reach your customers and grow your product |
| **ICP** | Ideal Customer Profile — a description of the perfect customer for your product |
| **JTBD** | Jobs-To-Be-Done — a way of thinking about customers: they "hire" your product to get a job done |
| **Lean Canvas** | A one-page business plan template that focuses on problems, solutions, and key metrics |
| **LLM** | Large Language Model — the AI technology behind tools like ChatGPT and Claude |
| **LTV** | Lifetime Value — the total amount of money a customer pays you from start to finish |
| **Magic Moment** | The first time a new user experiences real value from your product |
| **MRR** | Monthly Recurring Revenue — the total predictable revenue your product earns each month |
| **NPS** | Net Promoter Score — a 0–100 score measuring how likely customers are to recommend you |
| **PLG** | Product-Led Growth — a strategy where the product itself drives new sign-ups and upgrades |
| **PMF** | Product-Market Fit — the point where your product genuinely solves a real problem for a market |
| **SAM** | Serviceable Addressable Market — the part of the total market you can realistically reach |
| **SOM** | Serviceable Obtainable Market — the portion of SAM you'll realistically capture |
| **SWOT** | Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats — a framework for strategic analysis |
| **TAM** | Total Addressable Market — the full universe of potential customers worldwide |
| **Unit Economics** | The revenue and cost associated with a single customer unit |
