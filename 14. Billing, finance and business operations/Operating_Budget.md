# HOW TO FILL THIS DOCUMENT (READ THIS FIRST)

**What is this?** A money plan. It answers three questions: *How much will we spend? How much will we earn? Will we have enough money until our first 500 paying customers?*

**Who can fill it?** Anyone who can read and write numbers. If you can add, subtract and multiply, you can finish this.

**How long does it take?** About 60 to 90 minutes for a small project. Do not rush the questions marked ⭐.

## The 8 golden rules

1. **Work top to bottom.** Section 1, then 2, then 3, and so on. Later sections use numbers from earlier ones.
2. **Answer every question.** Never leave a blank. Write a real answer, or write `UNKNOWN` and add a line in Section 13 (Open Questions) saying who will find out and by when.
3. **Never guess silently.** If you are not sure, still write your best number, but mark the *Confidence* column:
   - ✅ **Sure** = I have a price list, quote or invoice.
   - 🟡 **Okay** = I looked at similar products or did simple math.
   - 🔴 **Guess** = I am making it up. (Allowed, but it must be firmed up before approval.)
4. **Use one currency and one unit of time.** Write your currency once in Section 1. Use that everywhere. Costs are **per month** unless the column says otherwise.
5. **Numbers only in number cells.** Write `1200`, not `about twelve hundred` and not `$1,2oo`. Put words in the *Notes* column.
6. **Show your math.** If you multiply or add, write how in the *Notes* column (example: `3 tools × 20 = 60`).
7. **Prices change.** For anything priced online (AI models, hosting, software), write the **date you checked the price** and the **link** in the Notes column.
8. **Do not delete questions.** If something does not apply, write `N/A` and one short reason (example: `N/A – we have no employees`).

## Words you might not know (quick dictionary)

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **Budget** | The amount of money we plan to spend. |
| **Operating budget** | The money needed to *run* the project day to day (tools, people, servers, ads, support). |
| **One-time cost** | You pay it once (example: buying a logo design). |
| **Monthly cost** | You pay it every month (example: hosting, software subscriptions). |
| **Variable cost** | A cost that goes up when you have more customers (example: AI usage, payment fees). |
| **Customer** | A person or company that pays you. A free user is *not* a customer. |
| **Runway** | How many months you can keep going before the money runs out. |
| **Burn** | Money going out each month, minus money coming in. |
| **Contingency** | Extra money saved for surprises. |
| **CAC** | "Customer Acquisition Cost". The money spent to win one new customer. |
| **Break-even** | The moment when money coming in equals money going out. |
| **Token** | A small piece of text that an AI model reads or writes. AI companies charge by the token. |
| **AI agent** | An AI helper that does tasks (writes code, tests, drafts text) with little supervision. |
| **Phase** | One stage of the project, from idea to launch to your first 500 customers. |

# 4. THE 6 PHASES: FROM IDEA TO YOUR FIRST 500 CUSTOMERS

*This is the heart of the budget. Each phase has: (a) questions, (b) a cost table, (c) a total. Fill in costs for **every** line. If a line does not apply, write `0` and a reason in Notes.*

**How to fill each cost table:**
- **One-time cost** = pay once. **Monthly cost** = pay every month.
- **Months** = how many months in this phase you will pay it.
- **Total for this phase** 🧮 = One-time + (Monthly × Months).
- **Confidence** = ✅ / 🟡 / 🔴.

# 5. AI COST ESTIMATOR

*Why this matters: AI is not charged like normal software. You pay per **token** (small pieces of text), and the **output** the AI writes usually costs more than the **input** you send it. The bill can swing a lot depending on the model you choose and how long the conversations are.*

> **Before you start:** Open the price page of each AI provider you plan to use. Write the price **per 1 million tokens** for input and for output, plus the **date you checked** and the **link**. Do not copy prices from memory. They change often.

**How to calculate "Cost per run" 🧮:**
`(input tokens ÷ 1,000,000 × input price) + (output tokens ÷ 1,000,000 × output price)`

> Example with made-up numbers: 2,000 input tokens at 3 per million and 500 output tokens at 15 per million = (2,000 ÷ 1,000,000 × 3) + (500 ÷ 1,000,000 × 15) = 0.006 + 0.0075 = **0.0135 per run**.

> ⚠️ **Hidden-cost reminder:** system instructions, tool definitions and earlier chat history are re-sent as input on every call. Agents that make many calls for one task cost much more than a single call. Add a safety multiplier in the Notes if unsure.
