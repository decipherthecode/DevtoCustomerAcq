# HOW TO FILL OUT THIS DOCUMENT (READ THIS FIRST)

> **Template version:** 1.1 (draft for review) | **Folder:** Customer feedback and product improvement
> **What this file is:** One file that plans every customer conversation for your product, from the first idea, through building and launch, until your **first 500 customers** have used it.
> **Who it is for:** A solo founder or product manager. You do not need to know research jargon. If you can answer a question in plain words, you can fill this in.

## The 60-second idea

Talking to customers is like asking a friend what they did *yesterday*, not what they *might do next year*. People are bad at predicting the future and great at telling true stories about the past. This plan helps you:

1. Decide **what you need to learn**.
2. Decide **who to ask** and **when**.
3. Ask in a way that gets **honest answers**.
4. Write down what you heard, **turn it into decisions**, and **tell customers what changed**.

## The 7 golden rules (stick these on your wall)

1. **Ask about the past, not the future.** Ask "Tell me about the last time you..." Never ask "Would you use...?"
2. **Talk about their life, not your idea.** Do not pitch during the interview.
3. **Listen more than you talk.** Aim for the customer to speak at least 80% of the time.
4. **Never lead.** Do not hint at the answer you want.
5. **Write it down the same day.** Memory fades fast.
6. **Get permission first.** Record only with clear consent (see Part 10).
7. **Every interview ends with a decision or a next question.** If nothing changes, you wasted the interview.

## Four ways to fill the blanks

- **Question and answer:** Read the question, write your answer under it.
- **Fill-in-the-blank:** Finish the sentence, like "We want to learn ______ from ______."
- **Checkboxes:** Tick everything that is true.
- **Tables:** Add one row per item. Copy the empty row if you need more.

## Rules for filling in the blanks

- ✅ Write **short, concrete** answers (one to three sentences). Numbers beat adjectives ("12 people" beats "many people").
- ✅ If you don't know yet, write **`UNKNOWN`**, then add *who will find out* and *by when*. Never leave a blank empty and never guess silently.
- ✅ Use the customer's **own words** in quotes when you can.
- ❌ Do not delete sections. If a section does not apply, write **`N/A, because ______`**.
- ❌ Do not put real names, emails, phone numbers, or any personal data in this file. Use IDs like `P-001` (see Part 10 and Part 11).

## Three ways to use this template

| Way | Best when | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Solo, by hand** | You like writing things down yourself | Fill the ✏️ lines yourself |
| **With an AI agent as your coach** | You want to go faster | Paste one part at a time and ask: "Ask me the questions in this part, one at a time, and write my answers into the template." Then **read every answer and correct it** |
| **With a teammate** | Two people share the plan | One fills, one reviews; use the tick-boxes in Part 14 to agree on "done" |

## PHASE 1: DISCOVER (before building anything)

**Plain-words goal:** Find out whether real people have this problem, how bad it is, and how they cope today.

**6.1.1 Our goal for this phase, in a sentence.** ✏️ ______
**6.1.2 Who we will interview and how many.** ✏️ Groups: ______ Number: ______ (suggested 10 to 15)
**6.1.3 Methods** (see Part 7) ☐ Video ☐ Phone ☐ In-person ☐ Async/written

**6.1.4 Question bank** (pick 8 to 10 for your script. Customize the brackets.)

*Opening and context*
- "Tell me a little about [what they do / their day-to-day]."
- "How long have you been doing [the activity]?"

*Story questions (the most valuable)*
- "Tell me about the **last time** you [did the thing]. Walk me through it from the start."
- "What was happening just before that?"
- "What did you do next? Then what?"

*Pain and workarounds*
- "What was the hardest part?"
- "What did you try to fix it? How did that go?"
- "Did you ever look for a tool, app, or person to help? What did you find?"
- "How much time or money did it cost you?"

*Importance*
- "Where does this rank among your other problems?"
- "What would happen if you did nothing about it?"

*Closing*
- "Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?"
- "Who else should I talk to about this?"

**6.1.5 Checklist of commitments** (real interest shows up as actions, not compliments). Ask yourself after each interview: did they ☐ give time ☐ introduce us to someone ☐ share real data or examples ☐ agree to a follow-up ☐ offer to pay or pre-order?

- ☐ We did at least ______ interviews (target 10+)

- ☐ We can name the **top 3 pains** in the customers' own words
- ☐ We know what people use **today** instead of our product
- ☐ We named the **first customer group** to serve (Part 4.2)
- ☐ At least ______ people took a **real action** (follow-up, introduction, deposit)
- ☐ Insights are logged in Part 13

# PART 8. THE INTERVIEW SCRIPT BUILDER

## 8.1 The 4-part shape of every interview

| Part | Time (30-min interview) | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Warm-up** | 3 to 5 min | Make them comfortable, explain the rules, ask for consent |
| **2. Story** | 15 to 20 min | Hear a real, recent story |
| **3. Deep dive** | 5 to 8 min | Dig into the moments that mattered |
| **4. Wrap-up** | 2 to 3 min | Thank them, ask for referrals and follow-up |

## 8.2 Opening words (copy and edit)

> "Thanks for making time. I'm learning about [topic] and I'm **not selling anything**. There are no right or wrong answers, and honest feedback, including negative, is the most helpful thing you can give. With your OK, I'd like to [record / take notes] so I don't miss anything. We can stop anytime. Is that alright?"

✏️ Our version: ______

## 8.3 Good questions vs bad questions

| ❌ Bad question | Why it's bad | ✅ Better question |
|---|---|---|
| "Would you use an app that reminds you to water plants?" | Asks about the future; invites a polite "yes" | "Tell me about the last time you forgot to water a plant." |
| "Do you like our design?" | Invites politeness | "What did you expect to happen when you tapped that?" |
| "How much would you pay for this?" | Guess, not behavior | "What do you spend on this problem today?" |
| "Don't you think it's too expensive?" | Leading | "How did you feel about the price when you saw it?" |
| "Do you have this problem often?" | Yes/no and vague | "When did it last happen? What happened before that?" |
| "What features do you want?" | Customers are poor at designing solutions | "What were you trying to get done when that was hard?" |

## 8.4 Follow-up phrases for any answer

- "Tell me more about that."  - "What happened next?"  - "Why was that important?"  - "Can you give me an example?"  - "What did you do about it?"  - "How did that make things better or worse?"  - Then **stay quiet for 5 seconds**. People fill silence with the truth.

# PART 18. SURVEYS AND METRICS (HEARING FROM MANY PEOPLE AT ONCE)

> **Plain words:** Interviews tell you **why**. Surveys and numbers tell you **how many**. Use both. Surveys are quick, but they are bad at discovering things you did not already know, so never use a survey instead of the interviews in Part 6.

## 18.1 Interview or survey? Pick the right tool

| You want to know... | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Why people do something | Interview | You can follow up |
| What problems exist that we haven't thought of | Interview | Surveys only ask what you already thought of |
| How many people feel something | Survey | Quick, same question to many |
| Whether a fix worked | Survey or product numbers | Compare before and after |
| Which of 3 known options people prefer | Survey | Easy to count |
| Why someone stopped using the product | Interview (short) | Needs the story |

## 18.2 Which surveys will we run, and when?

✏️ Tick what you will use. Write the moment it is sent and a target number of answers.

| Survey | What it asks (plain words) | Tick | Best phase | When it is sent | Target answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sign-up question** | "What made you sign up today?" (one open text box) | ☐ | 3, 4, 5 | At sign-up | ___ |
| **Disappointment question** | "How would you feel if you could no longer use this?" (Very / Somewhat / Not disappointed) | ☐ | 3, 4, 5 | After 2 or more uses | ___ |
| **Satisfaction (CSAT)** | "How satisfied are you with [this thing]?" (1 to 5) | ☐ | 3, 4, 5 | Right after a key action | ___ |
| **Effort (CES)** | "How easy was it to [do the task]?" (1 to 5) | ☐ | 2, 3 | Right after the task | ___ |
| **Recommend (NPS)** | "How likely are you to recommend us to a friend or colleague?" (0 to 10), then "Why?" | ☐ | 4, 5 | After 2 to 4 weeks of use | ___ |
| **Leaving survey** | "What is the main reason you are leaving?" (pick one + other) | ☐ | 3, 4, 5 | At cancel or after long inactivity | ___ |
| **Need-check survey** | "How often does [problem] happen to you?" (never / monthly / weekly / daily) | ☐ | 1 | Before building | ___ |

💡 *PlantPal:* Disappointment question after the 3rd use. Target: 40 answers by customer #100.
💡 *TeamSlot:* CES after a clinic books its first shift. Target: 10 answers from admins.

## 18.3 Survey rules (keep them honest)

☐ Keep it to **5 questions or fewer** (one is often best) ☐ Ask **one thing per question**
☐ Use the **same words** each time so results can be compared ☐ Offer an **"Other"** and a **"Not sure"** option
☐ Never ask leading questions ("How great was...") ☐ Tell people **how long** it takes and **why** you ask
☐ Do not ask for personal data you do not need (Part 10) ☐ Sending too many surveys makes people stop answering; **no more than 1 survey per person per month**
☐ Remember: **people who answer are not everyone.** Write down who might be missing.

## 18.4 How many answers do we need? (beginner rules of thumb)

| Goal | Rough guide |
|---|---|
| Spot big, obvious patterns | 30 or more answers |
| Compare two groups | 30 or more per group |
| Trust a percentage | 100 or more answers (still a rough guess) |

⚠️ A **score** (like NPS) from fewer than about 30 answers jumps around a lot. Look at **the comments**, not just the number.

# PART 19. PRICING AND WILLINGNESS-TO-PAY RESEARCH

> **Plain words:** Asking "Would you pay $10?" gets polite, unreliable answers. Better evidence is what people **already pay**, what they **do** when asked for money, and how they **react to real prices**.

## 19.1 When to run pricing research

| Phase | What to learn about price |
|---|---|
| 1 Discover | What do people spend today on this problem (money and time)? Who pays? |
| 2 Design and Build | Which kind of pricing sounds fair (subscription, one-time, per user)? Light test only |
| 3 Private Beta | Will anyone actually pay? Test a **real price** with real checkout or a pre-order |
| 4 Launch | Which plan do people pick, and why? What stops them from paying? |
| 5 Grow to 500 | Is the price right for each group? Who is under-paying or over-paying? |

## 19.2 Pricing questions (past behavior first)

*Today's spending*
- "What do you spend on this today, in money and in time?"
- "Who pays for it? Who has to approve it?" (B2B)
- "When did you last pay for something like this? How did you decide?"

*Reaction to a real price*
- "[Show the real price]. What is your first reaction? What goes through your mind?"
- "Compared with what you use now, how does this feel?"
- "What would make this feel clearly worth it? What would make it feel too much?"

*Buying process (B2B or internal tools)*
- "Who else would have to say yes? What happened the last time you bought a tool like this?"
- "Do you have a budget for this? Who owns it?"
- "How long does buying something like this usually take?"

⚠️ **Avoid:** "Would you pay $X?" · "Is $X too expensive?" · "What should we charge?"

## 19.3 The four-price-questions method (optional, use with a survey of 30 or more)

Ask each person these four questions about the product, in this order, and let them type a number:
1. At what price would it feel **so cheap** you would doubt its quality?
2. At what price does it feel like a **bargain**?
3. At what price does it start to feel **expensive**, but you would still think about it?
4. At what price is it **too expensive** to consider?

Look for the price range where "too cheap" and "too expensive" answers are both low. This gives a **range**, not a final price. Always confirm with real purchases (19.4).

|---|---|---|---|

| 4 | Agreed to a paid pilot or deposit | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ | ___ |

# PART 20. FROM INSIGHT TO DEV TICKETS AND AI CODING-AGENT TASKS

> **Plain words:** An insight only matters if it changes the product. This part turns what you heard into small, clear work items that a developer **or an AI coding agent** can pick up, then checks that the fix really helped.

## 20.1 The insight-to-product pipeline

| Step | Who | What happens | Done |
|---|---|---|---|

| 4 | 🧑 | Check the ticket against **Definition of Ready** (20.3) | ☐ |
| 5 | 🤖 or 🧑 | Build it (a developer or an AI coding agent using 20.4) | ☐ |

# GLOSSARY (PLAIN WORDS)

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Interview** | A friendly one-to-one conversation to learn about someone's real experience |
| **Persona / segment** | A group of similar customers |
| **Screener** | A few quick questions to check someone fits |
| **Hypothesis** | A guess that customers can prove right or wrong |
| **Prototype** | An early, unfinished version you can show people |
| **Beta** | A test version with a small group |
| **Churn** | When customers stop using the product |
| **Insight** | Something you learned that can change a decision |
| **Saturation** | When new interviews stop teaching you new things |
| **Synthesis** | Combining many interviews to find patterns |
| **Leading question** | A question that hints at the answer you want |
| **Consent** | Clear permission, given before you record or use someone's words |
| **De-identify** | Remove names and details that could identify a person |
| **Gate** | A checklist you must pass before going to the next phase |
| **AI agent** | A software helper that can do tasks like transcribing or summarizing |
| **NPS** | A 0 to 10 score of how likely people are to recommend you |
| **CSAT** | A 1 to 5 score of how satisfied people are with something specific |
| **CES** | A 1 to 5 score of how easy something was to do |
| **Activation** | When a new person reaches the first useful moment in the product |
| **Retention** | The share of people who keep using the product over time |
| **Willingness to pay** | How much someone is truly ready to spend, shown best by what they do |
| **Ticket** | A small, clear work item that says what to build or fix and why |
| **Acceptance check** | A yes/no statement that proves a ticket is done |
| **Definition of Ready** | A checklist a ticket must pass before anyone starts it |
| **Traceability** | A trail from what a customer said to what we changed |
