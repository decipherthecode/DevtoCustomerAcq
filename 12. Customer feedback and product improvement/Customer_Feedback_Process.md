# HOW TO FILL THIS DOCUMENT (READ THIS FIRST)

**What this file is:** One reusable template that guides you through collecting customer feedback, deciding what to do with it, and improving your product, from the day you start building until your first **500 customers** are using it.

**Who it is for:** A solo founder or builder. A beginner can fill it in. If you can answer a question out loud, you can write the answer here.

**How long it takes:** About 2-3 hours for the first full pass. Then 15 minutes per week to keep it alive.

## The 6 simple rules

1. **Copy this file for every new project.** Never write inside the master copy. Name your copy `Customer_Feedback_Process_[PRODUCT NAME].md`.
2. **Go top to bottom.** Each Part builds on the one before it.
3. **Replace everything in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]`.** Brackets mean "write your answer here". Delete the brackets afterwards.
4. **Read the *Example answer* under each question**, then write your own. Never copy the example. It only shows the style.
5. **Every section ends with a "Done when" line.** Tick the box only when the line is true.
6. **If you do not know an answer, write `UNKNOWN - [who/what will find out] - [date]`.** Never leave a blank and never guess. Come back to every UNKNOWN before you move to the next stage.

## How to read the symbols

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `[ ]` / `[x]` | Not done / Done |
| `[BRACKETS]` | You must fill this in |
| **Q:** | A question for you to answer |
| *Example answer:* | A sample of a good answer (do not copy it) |
| **Done when:** | The test that tells you the section is finished |
| 🤖 | A step an AI agent can do (a human still checks it) |
| 👤 | A step only a human should do |
| ⚠️ | Danger or common mistake |

## Quick-start path (if you only have 30 minutes)

Fill in **Part 1, Part 2, Part 4 and Part 5** only. These four Parts give you a working plan. Return later for the rest.

## What "done" looks like for this whole document

- [ ] No `[BRACKETS]` left, except in stages you have not reached yet.
- [ ] Every `UNKNOWN` has an owner and a date.
- [ ] Appendix C (Final Quality Check) is fully ticked.
- [ ] You can explain your feedback process to a friend in under two minutes.

# PART 8. DECIDING WHAT TO DO (MoSCoW PRIORITIZATION)

> You will never build everything. MoSCoW sorts every item into one of four buckets.

| Bucket | Meaning | Test question |
|---|---|---|
| **Must** | Without this, customers leave or the product is unsafe/broken | "Will we lose customers or trust if we skip this now?" |
| **Should** | Important, painful to skip, but there is a workaround | "Does it hurt many customers but not stop them?" |
| **Could** | Nice to have, small benefit | "Would it be pleasant but not missed?" |
| **Won't (this time)** | Not now, maybe never | "Does it stray from our goal or ideal customer?" |

## 8.1 How to decide (follow the order)

1. Is it S1 or S2, a legal/privacy problem, or a blocker to the aha moment? → **Must**
2. Do **[3 or more]** different customers ask for it, or does it affect a key metric in Part 2? → **Should**
3. Is it asked by 1-2 people, or does it improve polish? → **Could**
4. Does it not fit Q1.1 and Q1.4 (your product and ideal customer)? → **Won't**

**Q8.1 My rule for "how many customers make a pattern":** [e.g. 3 customers in 30 days]

**Q8.2 Capacity check. How many hours per week can you spend building?** [HOURS]
Rule: **Musts must fit in about 60% of your capacity, Shoulds about 30%, Coulds about 10%.** Never plan 100%. Leave room for surprises.

**Q8.3 Must-not-do list (things you will refuse even if asked):**
*Example answer:* "Custom features for one customer; features that require collecting more personal data than needed."
**Your list:** [LIST]

⚠️ Common mistakes: (1) building what the loudest person wants, (2) building what many say instead of what they do, (3) calling everything Must.

**Done when:** you can sort three real items into buckets in under 5 minutes. [ ]

# PART 9. AI-AGENT WORKFLOW

> AI helps you do four jobs faster. A human checks the work before it touches a customer or the product.

## 9.0 Rules for every AI job

1. **Never paste personal data** (names, emails, phone numbers, payment details) into an AI tool unless your privacy section (Part 14) allows it. Replace with `user_ID`.
2. **Keep the exact customer words.** The AI adds labels. It never replaces the original text.
3. **AI output is a draft.** A human reads before sending or building.
4. **Log mistakes.** If the AI got it wrong, write down how in [WHERE] and improve the prompt.
5. **Fixed output format.** Always ask the AI to answer in the same table so you can compare weeks.

## 9.1 Job A: Tag and categorize feedback 🤖

**When:** every new feedback item (or daily batch).

**Prompt template (copy, then fill the brackets once):**

```text
You are a feedback analyst for [PRODUCT NAME], which is [ONE-SENTENCE DESCRIPTION FROM Q1.1].
Ideal customer: [FROM Q1.4].

For each feedback item below, return a table with these columns:
ID | Type | Theme | Sentiment | Severity (S1-S4 or N/A) | Confidence (High/Med/Low) | One-line summary

Allowed Types: Bug, Usability issue, Feature request, Improvement, Question, Praise, Pricing/billing, AI quality, Churn reason, Other.
Allowed Themes: [YOUR THEMES FROM Q6.1].
Rules:
- Use only the allowed values.
- If unsure, say Low confidence. Do not guess.
- Do not rewrite the customer's words.
- Do not invent facts that are not in the item.

Feedback items:
[PASTE ITEMS WITH IDS, PERSONAL DATA REMOVED]
```

**Human check:** read all Low confidence rows and a random 10% of the rest. **Accuracy target:** [e.g. 90%+]. If below target, improve the prompt before trusting it.

## 9.2 Job B: Weekly insights digest 🤖

**When:** before your weekly review (Part 7.2).

```text
You are summarizing a week of customer feedback for [PRODUCT NAME] at Stage [1-6].
Input: the tagged feedback log for [DATE RANGE] and last week's digest.

Return in this exact format:
1. Top 5 themes by number of items (with counts and change vs last week)
2. Top 3 pain points with 2 exact customer quotes each (use IDs, no personal data)
3. New issues that did not exist last week
4. Anything Severity S1 or S2 (list first)
5. Positive signals (what customers love)
6. Churn reasons, if any
7. Three questions we should ask customers next
8. What you are NOT sure about

Rules: use only the provided data, do not speculate, show counts, keep it under 400 words.
```

**Human check:** open two original items behind each top theme to confirm the AI summarized correctly.

## 9.3 Job C: Draft replies and release notes 🤖

**Reply drafting prompt:**

```text
Draft a reply to this customer message for [PRODUCT NAME].
Tone: [TONE FROM Part 11].
Customer message: [MESSAGE, PERSONAL DATA REMOVED]
Known facts you may use: [STATUS FROM LOG, KNOWN WORKAROUND, ETA IF CONFIRMED]
Rules: thank them once, answer their actual question, never promise a date or feature that is not listed in "Known facts", under 120 words, no jargon.
```

**Release notes prompt:**

```text
Write release notes for version [X.Y] of [PRODUCT NAME] for non-technical customers.
Changes: [LIST FROM LOG, WITH FB IDS]
Format: "What's new", "What we fixed", "Thanks to your feedback" (mention themes, not names unless consent = Yes).
Rules: plain language, under 200 words, no promises about future releases.
```

**Human check:** 👤 a person sends every reply and publishes every release note. Check facts, tone and promises.

## 9.4 Job D: Turn feedback into specs and tickets 🤖

**When:** an item or group of items is marked **Must** or **Should**.

```text
Turn the feedback below into a build-ready work item.

Product context: [Q1.1 and Q1.4]
Feedback IDs and exact words: [PASTE]
Constraints: [TECH, TIME, PRIVACY RULES]

Return exactly:
1. Title (max 10 words)
2. Problem statement (who, what, why it hurts)
3. Evidence (IDs, counts, quotes)
4. Goal (one measurable result)
5. Proposed solution (smallest version that solves the problem)
6. Out of scope (what NOT to do)
7. Acceptance criteria (3-7 items, each testable: "Given... When... Then...")
8. Edge cases and error states
9. Privacy and security check (does it collect, store or share personal data?)
10. How we will measure success after release
11. Open questions for a human

Rules: do not invent requirements. Mark anything unknown as "UNKNOWN - needs human decision".
```

**Human check:** 👤 you approve the spec before building. If AI agents will build it, also fill the **Agent Task Card** in Part 10.2.
