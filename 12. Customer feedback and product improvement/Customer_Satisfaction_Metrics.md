# How to Fill In This Document (Read This First)

This is a **fill-in questionnaire**. You do not need to be a technical person. If you can read a question and write an answer, you can finish this document.

It helps you decide **how you will find out if customers are happy with your product**, from the day you start building until **your first 500 customers** have used it.

## The 8 Rules

1. **Go in order.** Start at Section 1 and keep going. Each section builds on the one before it.
2. **Answer every question marked `[REQUIRED]`.** Questions marked `[OPTIONAL]` can be skipped. Write `SKIPPED: <short reason>` so nobody wonders.
3. **Write your answer on the `Your answer:` line** (or in the table cell). Replace every `______` with your real answer.
4. **Never guess quietly.** If you do not know, write `UNKNOWN: <who can find out> by <date>`. Then add it to the **Open Questions list** in Section 17. An honest "I don't know" is better than a made-up answer.
5. **Use numbers, not feelings.** Write "80%" instead of "pretty good". Write "by 30 Nov 2026" instead of "soon".
6. **Examples are there to help you.** They look like this: *Example: ...*. Copy the style, not the words. You may delete examples when you are done.
7. **Tick boxes by changing `- [ ]` to `- [x]`.**
8. **One file only.** Everything about customer satisfaction for this project lives in this file. Do not split it up.

## Words You Will See (Mini Dictionary)

| Word | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Customer** | A person or company that uses your product. Section 3 defines exactly who counts. |
| **Metric** | A number that tells you something, like "how many customers are happy". |
| **CSAT** (Customer Satisfaction Score) | Asks "How happy are you with this?" Answered 1 to 5. |
| **NPS** (Net Promoter Score) | Asks "Would you recommend us to a friend?" Answered 0 to 10. |
| **CES** (Customer Effort Score) | Asks "Was that easy?" Answered 1 to 7. |
| **Retention** | How many customers stay with you over time. |
| **Churn** | How many customers leave. (Churn = 100% minus retention.) |
| **Activation** | The moment a customer first gets real value from your product. |
| **Cohort** | A group of customers who joined in the same week or month. |
| **Benchmark** | What is "normal" for other products. Use it as a hint, not a rule. |
| **Backlog** | The to-do list of things the team will build or fix. |
| **Detractor / Passive / Promoter** | The three NPS groups: unhappy (0-6), neutral (7-8), happy fans (9-10). |
| **Stage Gate** | A checkpoint. You must pass it before moving to the next stage. |
| **PII** | Personal information, such as a name, email or phone number. |
| **Consent** | The customer clearly said "yes, you may use my information for this reason". |

## The Journey This Document Covers

Customer satisfaction work happens in **six stages**. Each stage has its own questions.

| Stage | Name | What is happening | Customers involved |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S1** | Build | The product is being designed and built, with AI help. | Nobody outside the team yet |
| **S2** | Test | Quality checks, bug hunting, internal try-outs. | Team and test users |
| **S3** | Private Beta | A small invited group tries it. | About 5 to 50 invited people |
| **S4** | Launch | The product opens to the public. | First customers arrive |
| **S5** | Customers 1-100 | Early growth. Learn fast. | Customer #1 to #100 |
| **S6** | Customers 101-500 | Prove it works at larger scale. | Customer #101 to #500 |

**The finish line:** this document is "complete" for a project when **Customer #500** has used the product and the **Milestone Review** in Section 16 is signed.

## 7. Survey Design `[REQUIRED]`

*Why we ask: bad questions give bad answers. Keep surveys short and friendly.*

### 7.1 Survey Rules (Pre-Filled. Change only with a reason.)

- Ask **one thing per question**. Not "Was it fast and easy?" but two separate questions.
- Use the **exact wording** from Section 4. Do not rewrite it each time.
- Keep each survey to **3 questions or fewer** whenever possible, plus one optional comment box.
- Never ask a customer the same survey twice in 30 days.
- Do not ask leading questions ("How amazing was our wonderful app?").
- Always allow a customer to skip.
- Never pop up a survey while a customer is in the middle of a task.

## Appendix A: Formulas Cheat Sheet With Worked Examples

| Metric | Formula | Worked example |
|---|---|---|
| **CSAT %** | (answers of 4 or 5 ÷ all answers) × 100 | 80 answers; 50 are 4 or 5 → (50 ÷ 80) × 100 = **62.5%** |
| **NPS** | % Promoters (9-10) − % Detractors (0-6) | 100 answers: 60 promoters, 20 passives, 20 detractors → 60% − 20% = **+40** |
| **CES** | Average of all 1-7 answers | Answers 6, 5, 7, 4 → (6+5+7+4) ÷ 4 = **5.5** |
| **Response rate %** | (answers ÷ surveys sent) × 100 | 40 answers from 200 surveys → **20%** |
| **Activation %** | (customers who reached first success ÷ sign-ups) × 100 | 60 of 100 → **60%** |
| **Retention %** | (customers still active at end ÷ customers at start) × 100 | Start 200, still active 170 → **85%** |
| **Churn %** | 100 − Retention % | 100 − 85 = **15%** |
| **Feature adoption %** | (customers who used feature ÷ active customers) × 100 | 45 of 150 → **30%** |
| **Tickets per 100 customers** | (tickets ÷ customers) × 100 | 30 tickets, 250 customers → **12** |
| **NRR %** `[OPTIONAL]` | (starting revenue + upgrades − downgrades − cancellations) ÷ starting revenue × 100 | Start $1,000; +$150; −$50; −$100 → $1,000 ÷ $1,000 = **100%** |

**Reminder:** keep scales fixed (CSAT 1-5, NPS 0-10, CES 1-7) so your numbers can be compared across all your projects.

## Appendix B: Benchmarks (Typical Ranges, Checked Oct 2026)

**Read this before using any benchmark.** Published "averages" come from different companies, different survey methods and different industries. They are hints, not rules. Compare yourself to your **own trend** first.

| Metric | Commonly cited range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **CSAT** | 75-85% is generally "good"; above 80% is strong; staying below 70% is a sign to investigate | Most benchmarks assume a 1-5 scale. Support chats usually score higher than complex purchases. |
| **NPS** | Above 0 means more promoters than detractors. Many sources call 0-30 "good", above 50 "very strong", 70+ "world-class". SaaS averages are often quoted near +30 to +50. | Varies strongly by industry. Relationship NPS and after-support NPS are different measures. |
| **CES (1-7)** | 5 or above is generally considered good | Measure it right after the task. |
| **Early-stage SaaS monthly customer churn** | Reports for companies under about $1M annual recurring revenue range roughly from 5% to 8% per month depending on the study | Higher at the start because you are still finding the right customers. Watch the trend. |
| **Typical annual customer churn (private B2B SaaS)** | One 2026 startup report puts median annual customer churn near 16%; another puts median revenue churn near 12.5% | Strongly depends on price per customer and customer size. |
| **Annual retention of strong SaaS companies** | About 85-87% | Not a target for a brand-new product. |
| **Early churn** | Several reports say a large share of churn happens in the first 90 days. One benchmark says software products lose about 70% of users within three months. | This is **user** retention, not paid-subscription churn. Treat first-90-day behaviour as a top priority. |
| **Involuntary churn** | Reports suggest roughly 20-40% of subscription churn comes from failed payments, not unhappy customers | Fix payment retries before blaming the product. |

**How to use this:** copy the ranges you trust into Section 5.2, write the reason, and plan to reset them with your own data at customer #50.

## Appendix E: Quick Troubleshooting

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| "I don't know the answer." | Write `UNKNOWN: <who> by <date>` and add to Section 17. |
| "Nobody answers our surveys." | Shorten them, move them closer to the moment, offer a small thank-you, and ask personally (7.5). |
| "Only angry customers write to us." | Send a short CSAT to **all** customers at the key moment, not only those who complain. |
| "Our score jumped a lot in one week." | Check the number of answers (Q4.37) and whether tracking changed (Q9.3). |
| "CSAT is high but customers are leaving." | Trust behaviour more than words: look at activation, usage and cancel reasons. |
| "People disagree about who is a customer." | Re-read Q3.1 and Q3.2, update them, and record it in the Change Log. |
| "We need a number that isn't in this file." | Add it in Section 4.3 as an `Other` metric with a question, formula, timing and owner. |
| "A customer wants their data deleted." | Follow Q8.10 to Q8.13 and record the request. |
